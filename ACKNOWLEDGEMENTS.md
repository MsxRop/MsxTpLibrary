# Acknowledgements

Every software project is built from code.

The best projects, however, are built from people.

MSXTPLibrary would not exist without the inspiration, knowledge, encouragement and resources provided by many individuals, communities, authors and enthusiasts who have kept the MSX spirit alive for more than four decades.

I would like to express my sincere gratitude to all of them.

# MSX Italia

Special thanks to the MSX Italia Association and its members.

Through this community I rediscovered the MSX world, met passionate enthusiasts, shared ideas, received encouragement and, most importantly, made genuine friendships.

Although we live in different parts of Italy, we share the same enthusiasm for a computer platform that continues to inspire creativity and innovation.

# Mr Kazuhiko Nishi

My deepest respect and gratitude to Kazuhiko Nishi, creator of the MSX standard.

Without his original vision, none of this would exist.

Thank you also for your enthusiastic participation in the Pisa DevCon organized by MSX Italia, proving once again that the MSX spirit is still alive.

# Kari Lammassaari

Many thanks to Kari Lammassaari, whose libraries and programming work provided both inspiration and practical guidance during the development of this project.

Several ideas, techniques and even a few inline assembly solutions found their way into this library after studying his excellent work.

# MSX Pascal Project Template

Special thanks to the contributors of the project:

decocq-stuff/msx-pascal-project-template

This development environment has been invaluable throughout the creation and testing of MSXTPLibrary.

The integration with Visual Studio Code provides an efficient and comfortable modern workflow for Turbo Pascal development on the MSX platform.

# Edison Moraes

My sincere thanks to Edison Moraes for his remarkable book:

MSX Top Secret III

An extraordinary source of technical information, practical examples and inspiration for anyone interested in understanding the internals of the MSX architecture.

# C. I. Barkinshaw and R. Goodley

Many thanks to C. I. Barkinshaw and R. Goodley for their book:

MSX Machine Language

One of the few books available in Italian during the early years of the MSX, and a valuable introduction to the inner workings of the machine.

# Ian Sinclair

Special thanks to Ian Sinclair for the book:

MSX Machine Language and Assembly Language Programming

An excellent introduction to Z80 assembly language programming and an invaluable resource for understanding how the MSX hardware and BASIC interpreter operate internally.

# Jacopo Castelfranchi Editore

My appreciation goes to Jacopo Castelfranchi Editore, one of the very few Italian publishers that actively supported the MSX ecosystem during its golden years by publishing books and technical references for Italian users.

# Scuola 2F

A special mention goes to Scuola 2F which, perhaps without realizing it, introduced many students to what was arguably the only truly Italian MSX computer:

The Frael Bruc 100.

For some of us, that machine represented the beginning of a lifelong passion for computing.

# Microsoft Copilot

A personal thank you goes to Microsoft Copilot.

Every programmer knows that some of the best ideas arrive while explaining a problem to someone else. During the development of this library, Copilot often became that "someone else".

Many routines were born from long discussions about MSX internals, Turbo Pascal limitations, BIOS behaviour, graphics programming techniques and coding strategies. Sometimes the solution emerged immediately. More often it emerged after a long exchange of questions, attempts, mistakes and new ideas.

The final code is mine, as are all its strengths and all its flaws, but having an always-available technical companion made the journey considerably more enjoyable.

Perhaps this is one of the most unexpected aspects of the project: a library for a computer from the 1980s developed with occasional help from an artificial intelligence of the 2020s.

Computing has changed enormously over the years, but the pleasure of experimenting, learning and creating remains exactly the same.

The Global MSX Community

The MSX is more than a computer.

It is a community.

And this project is dedicated to that community.
